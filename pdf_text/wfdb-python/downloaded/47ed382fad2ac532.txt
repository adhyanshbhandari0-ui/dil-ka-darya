to ST exhibits a more gradual change in both HR and
MAP lacking the initial transient complex seen during
both RT and SU. We attribute this behavior to the slow
change in gravitational stress which essentially allows for
equilibration of the reflex responses during transition to
the head-up posture. This explanation is supported by
the fact that the hemodynamic response to ST becomes
indistinguishable from those seen during RT and SU about
50 seconds after the onset of gravitational stress which is
the time it takes for the slow tilt to be completed.
   Finally, a small pilot study suggests that the difference
between the hemodynamic responses seen during RT and
SU can be explained on the basis of muscle contraction.
Whether the large drop in MAP and the reflexive increase
in HR are mediated through peripheral vasodilation as
suggested by some [4, 5] or whether mechanical factors
such as transient changes in venous return due to abdominal
contraction play a dominant role remains to be elucidated.
